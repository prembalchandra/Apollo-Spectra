What is Robotic
Knee Replacement?

Robotic knee replacement is an advanced, computer-guided procedure in which an orthopaedic surgeon uses a robotic-assisted system to plan and perform selected steps of knee replacement with a high degree of precision.

The technology uses detailed information about the individual knee to support personalised surgical planning, bone preparation, alignment and implant positioning. Real-time guidance during surgery helps the surgeon carry out the planned procedure while accounting for the patient's knee anatomy.

The robotic system does not operate independently. The orthopaedic surgeon remains in control throughout the procedure and uses the technology as an assisting tool.

Robotic Knee Replacement Options

Not every damaged knee requires the same type of replacement. The orthopaedic surgeon assesses how much of the joint is affected before recommending the appropriate procedure.

Robotic Total Knee Replacement

Total knee replacement may be considered when arthritis or joint damage affects multiple areas of the knee. The damaged joint surfaces are replaced with artificial components to restore alignment and movement.

Robotic Partial Knee Replacement

Partial knee replacement may be suitable when damage remains limited to one compartment of the knee. Only the affected portion is replaced while suitable healthy parts of the joint are preserved.

Why Consider Robotic Knee Replacement Surgery?

Greater Precision

Detailed mapping and robotic guidance support accurate bone preparation, alignment and implant positioning.

Smaller Incision

Robotic-assisted techniques may allow more targeted surgical access with controlled handling of surrounding tissues.

Lesser Pain

Precise bone preparation and controlled tissue handling may help reduce postoperative discomfort in some patients.

Faster Recovery

Reduced tissue disruption and precise surgical execution may support early mobility and rehabilitation in suitable patients.

Minimal Scarring

A controlled surgical approach may help limit the extent of the surgical wound where clinically appropriate.

Shorter Hospital Stay

Some patients may be ready for discharge sooner once pain is controlled and they can move safely.
